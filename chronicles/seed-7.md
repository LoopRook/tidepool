# The pool of seed 7

*60 days, as recorded by the resident naturalist.*

- Day 1. So it begins. A Dappled Arrowtooth (Ocygnathus ocellatus) has eaten a Golden Wrackgill, and the pool is a little less innocent.
- Day 1. The Indigo-banded Tidesnouts (Halicephalus caeruleoannulatus) have learned the hard way that the Dappled Arrowtooths are hungry.
- Day 1. Twenty-five Red-spotted Beadfins and counting.
- Day 1. Counted twenty-five Golden Wrackgills today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 1. The Red-spotted Beadfins (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus) have learned the hard way that the Dappled Arrowtooths are hungry.
- Day 1. Fifty Golden Wrackgills and counting.
- Day 2. Fifty Red-spotted Beadfins and counting.
- Day 2. Hard times for the Indigo-banded Tidesnouts (Halicephalus caeruleoannulatus): barely five remain of the seventeen I counted recently.
- Day 2. Over a hundred Golden Wrackgills today. The pool is starting to look like theirs.
- Day 3. No Indigo-banded Tidesnouts left in the pool. Two days from first sighting to last; at their height there were 17.
- Day 4. There is a Dappled Arrowtooth out there with ten kills to its name. I have started giving it a wide berth, figuratively.
- Day 6. No Plain Peergills left in the pool. Five days from first sighting to last; at their height there were 14.
- Day 6. First recorded loss among the Plum Driftfangs (Segnipinna porphyrea): one went down to a Dappled Arrowtooth.
- Day 7. The Red-spotted Beadfins have crashed, from 41 to 14 in three days.
- Day 7. There is a Dappled Arrowtooth out there with twenty kills to its name. I have started giving it a wide berth, figuratively.
- Day 8. Counted twenty-five Dappled Arrowtooths today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 9. A Plum Driftfang ate one of its own. Hunger makes poor manners.
- Day 12. Something has gone wrong for the Red-spotted Beadfins. Five left, down from 17.
- Day 12. Struck the Red-spotted Beadfin (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus) from the list of the living. Twelve days, and at best fifty-eight of them.
- Day 12. Cannibalism among the Dappled Arrowtooths (Ocygnathus ocellatus). I will assume times are hard.
- Day 14. There is a Dappled Arrowtooth out there with thirty-five kills to its name. I have started giving it a wide berth, figuratively.
- Day 15. Generation 10. I have long since stopped drawing family trees.
- Day 22. The first death from old age: a Dappled Arrowtooth (Ocygnathus ocellatus), aged twenty-two days.
- Day 28. One Plum Driftfang has now made fifty kills, the most of any creature I have followed.
- Day 30. A venerable Dappled Arrowtooth has died at twenty-two days. Nothing in the pool has lived longer.
- Day 39. The pool's first new species: the Indigo-spotted Dusksail (Umbripinna caeruleomaculata), split from the Golden Wrackgill (Herbichthys flavus). It has taken to wearing spots.
- Day 39. A Dappled Arrowtooth was seen taking an Indigo-spotted Dusksail, the first of that kind I have seen fall to a predator.
- Day 40. I have stopped looking for the Indigo-spotted Dusksail (Umbripinna caeruleomaculata); it is extinct. A run of three days, peaking at 5.
- Day 45. I am now fairly sure the Dappled Gloamer (Scotopteryx guttata) is its own species, an offshoot of the Golden Wrackgill. It has taken to wearing spots.
- Day 46. The Dappled Gloamers (Scotopteryx guttata) have learned the hard way that the Dappled Arrowtooths are hungry.
- Day 48. The Plum Driftfang (Segnipinna porphyrea) has died out after forty-eight days. It never managed more than fifteen.
- Day 50. I have stopped looking for the Dappled Gloamer (Scotopteryx guttata); it is extinct. A run of eight days, peaking at 8.

## At the close

187 creatures alive, 3 of 13 recorded species surviving, deepest lineage 22 generations. The commonest creature is the Golden Wrackgill (*Herbichthys flavus*), 154 strong.
