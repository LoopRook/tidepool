# The pool of seed 7

*60 days, as recorded by the resident naturalist.*

- Day 1. So it begins. A Dappled Arrowtooth (Ocygnathus ocellatus) has eaten a Golden Wrackgill, and the pool is a little less innocent.
- Day 1. The Indigo-banded Tidesnouts (Halicephalus caeruleoannulatus) have learned the hard way that the Dappled Arrowtooths are hungry.
- Day 1. Twenty-five Red-spotted Beadfins and counting.
- Day 1. The Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus) now number twenty-five, a new high.
- Day 1. First recorded loss among the Red-spotted Beadfins (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus): one went down to a Plum Driftfang.
- Day 1. Counted fifty Red-spotted Beadfins today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 2. The Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus) have reached fifty. The algae may have opinions about this.
- Day 2. I counted a hundred Golden Wrackgills before giving up and calling it a hundred.
- Day 3. No Indigo-banded Tidesnouts left in the pool. Three days from first sighting to last; at their height there were 15.
- Day 3. One Plum Driftfang has now made ten kills, the most of any creature I have followed.
- Day 5. Cannibalism among the Plum Driftfangs (Segnipinna porphyrea). I will assume times are hard.
- Day 6. A Dappled Arrowtooth was seen taking a Plum Driftfang, the first of that kind I have seen fall to a predator.
- Day 6. There is a Plum Driftfang out there with twenty kills to its name. I have started giving it a wide berth, figuratively.
- Day 6. The Red-spotted Beadfins have crashed, from 43 to 15 in two days.
- Day 8. The last Plain Peergill (Argonectes levis) is gone. It lasted seven days and never numbered more than 14.
- Day 8. The Dappled Arrowtooths (Ocygnathus ocellatus) have reached twenty-five. Their prey may have opinions about this.
- Day 14. One Plum Driftfang has now made thirty-five kills, the most of any creature I have followed.
- Day 14. Generation 10. I have long since stopped drawing family trees.
- Day 17. The Red-spotted Beadfin (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus) has died out after sixteen days. Sixty-two at its peak, which is a respectable showing.
- Day 17. Cannibalism among the Dappled Arrowtooths (Ocygnathus ocellatus). I will assume times are hard.
- Day 21. A Golden Wrackgill died of old age at twenty days, the first in the pool to simply run out of years.
- Day 29. The oldest creature I have yet recorded, a Plum Driftfang of twenty-three days, died quietly today.
- Day 31. The Plum Driftfang (Segnipinna porphyrea) has died out after thirty days. It never managed more than fifteen.
- Day 54. The pool's first new species: the Dusky Mossgleaner (Phytopterus niger), split from the Golden Wrackgill (Herbichthys flavus). It is a different colour altogether.
- Day 54. A Dappled Arrowtooth was seen taking a Dusky Mossgleaner, the first of that kind I have seen fall to a predator.

## At the close

128 creatures alive, 3 of 18 recorded species surviving, deepest lineage 17 generations. The commonest creature is the Golden Wrackgill (*Herbichthys flavus*), 77 strong.
