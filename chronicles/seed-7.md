# The pool of seed 7

*60 days, as recorded by the resident naturalist.*

- Day 1. So it begins. A Dappled Arrowtooth (Ocygnathus ocellatus) has eaten an Indigo-banded Tidesnout, and the pool is a little less innocent.
- Day 1. First recorded loss among the Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus): one went down to a Dappled Arrowtooth.
- Day 1. Counted twenty-five Red-spotted Beadfins today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 1. The Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus) now number twenty-five, a new high.
- Day 1. Noted: the Plum Driftfangs hunt. One was seen making off with a Golden Wrackgill.
- Day 1. The Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus) have reached fifty. The algae may have opinions about this.
- Day 2. Counted fifty Red-spotted Beadfins today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 2. The Golden Wrackgills (Herbichthys flavus) now number a hundred. At this point they are less a species than a weather system.
- Day 3. No Indigo-banded Tidesnouts left in the pool. Two days from first sighting to last; at their height there were 12.
- Day 5. Something has gone wrong for the Red-spotted Beadfins. Nineteen left, down from 55.
- Day 5. One Dappled Arrowtooth has now made ten kills, the most of any creature I have followed.
- Day 6. The Dappled Arrowtooths (Ocygnathus ocellatus) now number twenty-five, a new high.
- Day 6. Hard times for the Red-spotted Beadfins (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus): barely seven remain of the twenty-one I counted recently.
- Day 7. The last Plain Peergill (Argonectes levis) is gone. It lasted six days and never numbered more than 10.
- Day 7. Struck the Red-spotted Beadfin (Pusillichthys rubroocellatus) from the list of the living. Seven days, and at best fifty-five of them.
- Day 9. Counted twenty-five Plum Driftfangs today; they are doing well for themselves.
- Day 13. Somewhere in the pool, a 10th-generation hatchling. The founders would hardly know it.
- Day 15. The Dappled Arrowtooths have crashed, from 26 to 9 in three days.
- Day 22. Fifty Plum Driftfangs and counting.
- Day 23. Struck the Dappled Arrowtooth (Ocygnathus ocellatus) from the list of the living. Twenty-three days, and at best thirty-six of them.
- Day 31. A Golden Wrackgill died of old age at seventeen days, the first in the pool to simply run out of years.
- Day 32. Cannibalism among the Plum Driftfangs (Segnipinna porphyrea). I will assume times are hard.
- Day 36. It has happened at last. The Greater Coral Reaver (Carnognathus roseus) has branched off from the Plum Driftfang (Segnipinna porphyrea). It is noticeably larger than its forebears.
- Day 37. Noted: the Greater Coral Reavers hunt. One was seen making off with a Golden Wrackgill.
- Day 37. The Smooth Needlefin (Tenuipinna concolor) has been breeding true since day 36; a new branch off the Golden Wrackgill.
- Day 43. Something has gone wrong for the Smooth Needlefins. Seven left, down from 21.
- Day 44. The Plum Driftfangs have crashed, from 36 to 11 in two days.
- Day 46. Entered a new species in the notebook: the Giant Inky Gloamfin (Umbripteryx atra), daughter of the Golden Wrackgill. It is noticeably larger than its forebears.
- Day 47. The Giant Inky Gloamfins (Umbripteryx atra) have learned the hard way that the Greater Coral Reavers are hungry.
- Day 57. A venerable Golden Wrackgill has died at nineteen days. Nothing in the pool has lived longer.
- Day 58. Old age took a Golden Wrackgill today, at a remarkable twenty-one days.

## At the close

134 creatures alive, 9 of 20 recorded species surviving, deepest lineage 22 generations. The commonest creature is the Golden Wrackgill (*Herbichthys flavus*), 95 strong.
